# Source-Derived Meru Reconstruction Constraints — v0.6

## Purpose

This document records geometrical constraints explicitly recoverable from the
primary Meru Foundation sources before any new three-dimensional model is
fitted.

The constraints are separated from later mathematical inference.

## C01 — The turn-count revision does not identify a new curve

The archived note to *The Light in the Meeting Tent* states that the form
originally described as a \(3\frac12\)-turn vortex was later counted as a
three-turn vortex.

It explicitly says that the vortex remained the same and that the counting
convention changed.

### Consequence

The corresponding half-form should not initially be split into independent
\(1\frac34\)-turn and \(1\frac12\)-turn model families.

## C02 — The original construction is a ribbon on a toroidal surface

The early source describes seven cyclic regions arranged on a ribbon carved on
the surface of a 2-torus.

### Consequence

A centreline-only curve is insufficient for historical reconstruction. The
eventual model must distinguish:

- the supporting surface;
- the ribbon centreline;
- ribbon width;
- ribbon orientation or twist.

## C03 — The half-ribbon runs from a central singularity to the rim

The source describes half of the ribbon as extending from a singularity near
the centre or axle of the toroidal form to its circumferential rim.

### Consequence

A conventional ring-torus surface has no literal surface point corresponding
to an axle singularity.

The historical surface is therefore likely pinched, dimpled, degenerate at one
location, or used in a non-standard topological drawing.

## C04 — Seven toroidal phases correspond to seven tetrahedral axes

The source associates seven ribbon regions or phases with:

- four vertex-to-opposite-face axes;
- three pairs of opposite edges.

### Consequence

The source-derived viewing protocol may involve seven axis families rather
than only the 12 elements of the proper tetrahedral rotation group.

## C05 — The viewing protocol contains both discrete and cyclic operations

The tetrahedral frame is described as being:

1. tipped through a discrete symmetry set; and
2. spun or rolled cyclically on edges and axes.

### Consequence

The v0.5 result remains exact for the finite proper tetrahedral group:

\[
12\rightarrow6\rightarrow3.
\]

It does not by itself exhaust a protocol containing an additional continuous
or discretely sampled roll parameter

\[
\alpha
\]

around a selected tetrahedral axis.

A later protocol may therefore require transformations of the form

\[
R_{a}(\alpha)R_i,
\]

where \(R_i\) is a discrete tetrahedral rotation and \(R_a(\alpha)\) is a
rotation around one of the source-defined axes.

The permitted values of \(\alpha\) remain unspecified.

## C06 — The alphabet claim is broader than a finite 12-view claim

The source claims that a single three-dimensional form produces alphabetic
shadow projections from different perspectives.

It does not provide a complete finite orientation table or state that one
letter corresponds to each of exactly 12 proper tetrahedral rotations.

### Consequence

The source-derived protocol must be recovered before the 22-letter claim can
be compared with a mathematically fixed orientation set.

## C07 — The standard (3,10) ring is transformed

The separate torus-knot poster describes the standard ring form of the
\((3,10)\) torus knot as being transformed to fit a dimpled-sphere torus.

### Consequence

The canonical v0.4 knot is a reference object, not the final source-derived
geometry.

The unknown transformation must be treated explicitly:

\[
F_{\mathrm{Meru}}:
\gamma_{3,10}^{\mathrm{ring}}
\longrightarrow
\gamma_{3,10}^{\mathrm{dimpled}}.
\]

## C08 — Several historical model families may coexist

The sources contain at least:

- an early toroidal ribbon or vortex;
- a flame in a tetrahedral vessel;
- a dimpled-sphere \((3,10)\) knot;
- a tetrahelix;
- a later hand-like physical model.

### Consequence

These must remain separate model versions until a source explicitly supplies
a transformation or equivalence between them.

## Immediate reconstruction boundary

The next stage may recover two-dimensional landmarks from source images, but a
unique three-dimensional reconstruction is not yet justified.

The first source-derived coordinate work should therefore record:

- visible surface outlines;
- dimple or singularity locations;
- visible centreline segments;
- occlusion boundaries;
- tetrahedral vertices and edges;
- start/end regions;
- apparent axis directions;
- image uncertainty.

No landmark extracted from a single perspective will be treated as a complete
three-dimensional coordinate.
