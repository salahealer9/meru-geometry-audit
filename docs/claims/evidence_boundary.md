# Evidence Boundary

## Purpose

This project investigates the mathematical, geometrical, combinatorial, and
statistical content of Stan Tenen's Meru Foundation research.

The initial objective is reconstruction and audit, not advocacy or dismissal.

## Initially in scope

- The reciprocal spiral \(r=a/\theta\).
- Alternative turn counts described in different Meru sources.
- Conventional torus embeddings.
- Dimpled spherical-torus or apple-like surface models.
- Regular tetrahedral geometry.
- The rotational symmetry group of the regular tetrahedron.
- Orthographic and, where historically justified, perspective projection.
- Ribbon, flame, and hand-like model variants.
- Claimed Hebrew-letter silhouettes.
- The 27 Hebrew letter forms and ternary triplets in \(\{0,1,2\}^3\).
- Spatial arrangements of Genesis 1:1.
- The claimed \((3,10)\) torus-knot construction.
- Objective shape metrics.
- Matched geometric null models.
- Comparisons with control alphabets and pseudo-alphabets.

## Not initially in scope

- Extraterrestrial authorship.
- Ancient-contact narratives.
- Claims about the Lord's Prayer referring to beings in outer space.
- Theological truth claims.
- Claims that particular words directly create matter or life.
- General claims about consciousness that lack an operational measurement.

These interpretations may be documented historically, but they will not be
treated as consequences of a successful geometric reconstruction.

## Central research question

Does the geometry described by Stan Tenen provide a sufficiently specified
three-dimensional generator whose constrained projections reproduce the Hebrew
alphabet significantly better than matched geometric controls?

## Interpretation rule

Even a successful common-generator result would establish only a geometrical
relationship under the tested reconstruction.

It would not independently establish:

- who designed the alphabet;
- when the proposed geometry originated;
- whether the relationship was intentional;
- whether it has extraterrestrial origins;
- whether language has anomalous physical effects.

## Reconstruction rule

Where an original source leaves a choice unspecified, the project will:

1. record the missing definition;
2. implement the simplest reasonable baseline;
3. label the choice as an inference;
4. test alternative plausible choices;
5. avoid presenting any inferred choice as Tenen's exact construction.

## Statistical rule

Any free search over model parameters, transformations, viewing orientations,
fonts, historical scripts, or scoring functions must be included in the null
procedure or separated into exploratory and confirmatory stages.
