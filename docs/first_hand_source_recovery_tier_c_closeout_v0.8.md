## 2. Federal court decision

The published federal decision:

*Tenen v. Winter*, 15 F. Supp. 2d 270 (W.D.N.Y. 1998)

identifies the object at issue as Tenen's three-dimensional sculpture used to cast flame-letter shadowgrams.

It also states that *The Light in the Meeting Tent* was registered with the U.S. Copyright Office together with an additional sheet under two registration numbers in 1992.

The decision does not disclose a deterministic FIRST HAND construction.

Importantly, at the pretrial stage the court stated that the factual question whether the competing Winter shape could actually be produced mathematically would require expert testimony.

Therefore the decision is evidence of:

- litigation chronology;
- copyrighted-work provenance;
- the identity of the object being disputed.

It is not an independent mathematical validation of Tenen's construction.

## 5. Copyright deposit does not imply enablement

U.S. Copyright Office guidance for visual arts states that three-dimensional sculptural works are represented by identifying material, such as:

- photographic prints;
- transparencies;
- photocopies;
- drawings;
- similar two-dimensional reproductions/renderings.

Thus:

> **A sculpture copyright registration does not imply that the deposit contains a patent-style enabling mathematical construction.**

The actual 1992 Tenen sculpture deposit/identifying material was not obtained in this checkpoint.

State:

`ARCHIVAL_DEPOSIT_NOT_OBTAINED_TIER_D_OPTIONAL`.

This does not block the Tiers A-C published-corpus result.

## 7. Legal record and the geometry audit

The legal record adds an important interpretive boundary.

The object protected in the litigation was the sculptural expression / depictions.

That does not establish that the sculpture was a purely mathematical object with a unique formula.

Indeed, the court record itself treats the question of whether a competing shape could be generated mathematically as a factual matter that would require expert evidence.

Therefore:

\[
\text{copyrighted sculpture}
\neq
\text{published enabling mathematical construction}.
