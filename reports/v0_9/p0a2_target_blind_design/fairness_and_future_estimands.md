## Candidate-minus-null contrast by capacity and interaction

For a future null law \(\mathcal P_r\), define a possible population contrast

\[
C_{c,r,k} = S_{c,k}-\mathbb E_{N\sim\mathcal P_r}[S_{N,k}],
\quad
\widehat C_{c,r,k}=S_{c,k}-B_r^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{B_r}S_{N_{r,j},k}.
\]

Lower-is-closer means negative C favors the candidate on that declared aggregate. B_r and the law are symbolic, not chosen. Paired object reuse across tiers must be declared so dependence is retained. An object×capacity interaction can be

\[
I_{c,r;k,0}
=[S_{c,k}-S_{c,0}]
-\mathbb E_{N\sim\mathcal P_r}[S_{N,k}-S_{N,0}]
=C_{c,r,k}-C_{c,r,0}.
\]

This distinguishes a candidate's capacity gain from controls' gain; monotone improvement alone does not establish specificity. Use the same observations and nuisance contract across tiers. Switching width/normalization makes the difference a combined methodological change, not a capacity-only interaction.

## Cross-candidate per-letter coherence

Define the symbolic advantage vector \(a_{c,r,k,\ell}=\mathbb E_r[\mathsf E_{N,k,\ell}]-\mathsf E_{c,k,\ell}\). A possible descriptive coherence measure between candidates c,d is the fixed-corpus centered inner product divided by the two centered vector norms (a correlation of their advantage patterns). This is undefined if either norm is zero, and is **not selected as primary**. A vector-level concordance/overlap functional could instead be preregistered. Report full per-letter profiles rather than choose favorable letters/candidates. Similar patterns need not mean small errors, independent evidence or historical causation. Shared chart error, common nulls and related candidate models induce dependence. No IID-letter standard errors or tests are justified by this descriptive comparison.

## Capacity-conditioned null calibration

A future conditional reference distribution is
\[
F_{r,k,q,F}(s\mid\mathcal T,\mathcal O,\mathcal B)
=\Pr_{N\sim\mathcal P_r}\{S^{q,F}_{N,k}\le s
\mid\text{fixed corpus, observation contract and full budget}\}.
\]

The symbolic empirical CDF uses the same declared null law and fixed conditions. A tail rank of S_c is a null-relative descriptive calibration unless a valid inferential null/exchangeability or other sampling argument is supplied. Under a separately proved exchangeability design, a possible finite-Monte-Carlo lower-tail p-value is \((1+\#\{j:S_{N_j,k}\le S_{c,k}\})/(B_r+1)\), with conservative tie handling. This formula is **conditional**, not a claim that a fixed historical candidate is exchangeable with arbitrary simulated curves. Numerical uncertainty spanning a comparison requires a registered interval/tie policy, not preferential rounding.

## Detectability, multiplicity and negative outcomes

Let Δ denote a scientifically interpretable advantage under a **future specified** alternative family \(\mathcal Q_{r,k,\Delta}\). With frozen test \(\varphi\), α, desired power 1−β, uncertainty model and B, a possible minimum detectable advantage is
\[
\Delta_{\min,r,k}
=\inf\{\Delta\ge0:\Pr_{\mathcal Q_{r,k,\Delta}}(\varphi\text{ rejects})\ge1-\beta\}.
\]

The set may be empty, nonmonotone or undefined without further assumptions; no detectability number follows from 22 letters or P0A0. Analytic power bounds or any simulation would require a **separately authorized** target-free design phase; none occurs here.

Finite Monte Carlo resolution is conditional on B (for the exchangeable rank example, 1/(B+1)); quantile/tail uncertainty needs its own coverage bound and stopping rule, with dependence/coupling explicitly handled. No α, B, adjustment method or stopping count is chosen here.

Nominal frame counts and repeated views of the same object are not independent trials. The 22 basic letters are fixed, not IID draws from a population of alphabets.
